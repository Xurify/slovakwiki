# Slovak Wiki: Lessons, Practice, and Reference

## Product position

Slovak Wiki is a reference-first site for English-speaking people who are actively learning Slovak. It helps them understand the language, use it in context, and return to what they need to retain.

The product is not a gamified course platform, a generic quiz bank, or a teacher dashboard.

## Site structure

Primary navigation:

- **Lessons** — focused, independent study units.
- **Practice** — contextual retrieval and review of items the learner has encountered.
- **Reference** — Dictionary, Grammar, Pronunciation, and language terms.

The home page remains quiet and points a new learner to a sensible first lesson and a returning learner to a useful next action. It does not become an Insights dashboard.

## Lessons

Lessons teach a single usable outcome in 10–15 minutes. They are freely browseable, with a suggested next unit but no locks, XP, streaks, levels, or prerequisite gates.

The Lessons landing page uses three restrained, typography-first track cards:

1. **Everyday Slovak** — situations a learner is likely to encounter.
2. **Grammar** — specific language moves used in real Slovak.
3. **Pronunciation** — sound and spelling patterns.

Cards lead to plain lesson lists. They are not a course grid and do not use decorative top bars, illustrations, or invented metrics.

### Lesson shape

Each lesson begins directly with a title, a useful promise, and a scene. It contains only the pieces needed for the stated outcome:

1. A short Slovak scene, dialogue, or authentic situation.
2. Meaning and 4–6 key words/forms.
3. One pattern worth understanding.
4. Two or three guided Slovak interactions.
5. A small real-use prompt.
6. A quiet recommended next lesson.

Every Slovak model sentence, key phrase, and practice prompt has audio. Dialogues use two consistent voices; standalone examples use one. Speaking practice is initially unscored: listen, then say it aloud.

### Everyday Slovak: first units

The initial browseable sequence is:

1. Meet someone
2. Numbers and personal details
3. Days, dates, and time
4. At a café
5. Getting around town
6. Shopping

`Core tools` is a visible group within Everyday Slovak, not a fourth track. The first three units make numbers, dates, times, and personal details explicit rather than treating them as incidental vocabulary.

### Grammar lessons

Grammar is practiced as Slovak, not tested as English terminology.

Example:

- Situation: talking about yourself.
- Prompt: repair `Ja číta knihu.`
- Learner writes: `Ja čítam knihu.`
- Feedback: `číta` means he/she reads; `čítam` is the first-person form.
- New use: complete a different first-person sentence.

The Grammar track can use academic titles when they have a precise utility subtitle, such as `Nominative case — name who or what performs an action`.

## Practice

Practice is returning usage, not a second set of lessons and not a generic quiz section.

It has two entry points:

- **Review now** — items the learner missed or revealed earlier.
- **Practice a topic** — a completed lesson or an explicitly saved reference item.

An item is contextual and focused:

`situation → Slovak task → Check or Reveal answer → correction + exact why → new use`

Typed correction, completion, and short construction are preferred when they test a form. Choice tasks are used only when choosing is the real skill. Every typed item has an explicit **Reveal answer** action. Revealed and missed items are added to Review.

The system records only learning state necessary for Review. It does not expose a separate Insights page, activity dashboard, score theatre, lives, leaderboards, or streaks.

## Reference

Reference is the durable source of truth. It explains terms, rules, tables, exceptions, and linked concepts. It does not copy whole lessons or turn every page into practice.

Reference pages can include:

- worked examples labelled with what they demonstrate;
- a link to the relevant lesson;
- a focused `Practice this` action on a specific word, form, or example.

They must not offer a broad, vague action such as practising an entire `Cases overview` page. `Chcem kávu → kávu after chcem` is an appropriate practice target; `Cases` is not.

Major subjects such as cases may also include a recommended study path. A path suggests order without gating research or navigation.

## Initial content map

The first public content should be small, complete, and mutually reinforcing. A phrase introduced in a lesson becomes a Review candidate only when a learner misses it or chooses to reveal it.

### Everyday Slovak

| Unit | You can now… | Scene and language focus | Grammar bridge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Meet someone | greet someone, say who you are, and ask where they are from | `Dobrý deň. Volám sa …`, `Teší ma.`, `Odkiaľ ste?`, `Som z …`, `Hovoríte po slovensky?` | `byť` and formal `Vy` |
| Numbers and personal details | give an age, phone number, address number, and simple price | `Mám dvadsať rokov.`, `Moje číslo je …`, `Koľko to stojí?` | numbers 1–4 and noun gender; numbers 5+ |
| Days, dates, and time | arrange a simple meeting | `Dnes je pondelok.`, `Stretneme sa v utorok.`, `O tretej.`, `O pol tretej.` | `v` with days; `o` for appointment time |
| At a café | order a drink or food politely and pay | `Prosím si kávu.`, `Dám si čaj.`, `Účet, prosím.`, `Môžem platiť kartou?` | a useful object form: `káva → kávu` |
| Getting around town | ask where something is and understand a basic destination | `Kde je stanica?`, `Ako sa dostanem do …?`, `Idem do mesta.`, `Som v meste.` | destination vs. location: `do + genitive` / `v + locative` |
| Shopping | ask for an item, a quantity, or a different size | `Hľadám …`, `Máte …?`, `Potrebujem väčšiu veľkosť.`, `Päť eur.` | quantity phrases and high-frequency object forms |

### Grammar

| Unit | You can now… | Slovak production task | Reference bridge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `byť` in the present | say who or what something is | repair `Ja je študent.` → `Som študent / študentka.` | `byť` forms and subject pronouns |
| Present-tense verb endings | say who is doing something now | repair `Ja číta knihu.` → `Čítam knihu.` | present tense |
| Naming the subject | spot and use the nominative in a real sentence | choose the person performing an action, then write a new sentence | nominative case |
| Useful object forms | make a direct request or say what you want | repair `Chcem káva.` → `Chcem kávu.` | accusative case |
| Where versus where to | distinguish a place from a destination | complete `Som v meste.` / `Idem do mesta.` | locative and genitive with prepositions |

### Pronunciation

| Unit | You can now… | Visible or audible focus | Reference bridge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Read the Slovak alphabet | read common Slovak spelling without treating it as English | `č, š, ž, ch, ľ, ď, ť, ň` | alphabet and spelling |
| Find the stress | place ordinary stress at the start of a word | `Bratislava`, `prosím`, `ďakujem` | first-syllable stress |
| Hear vowel length | notice when a long vowel changes the written word | `a/á`, `e/é`, `i/í`, `o/ó`, `u/ú`, `y/ý` | vowel length |

## Boundaries

- Do not copy textbook text, exercises, images, or audio from Krížom-krážom, slovake.eu, or other sources.
- Use their situation-first progression and input-to-practice structure as inspiration only.
- Czech–Slovak notes appear only when they prevent a real confusion and never become a primary focus.
- Do not add Classroom or educator tools at launch. That is a separate future product concern.
