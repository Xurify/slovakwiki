# Morphology Reference

Overview only. Full paradigms: [nouns.md](nouns.md), [adjectives-pronouns-numerals.md](adjectives-pronouns-numerals.md), [verb-forms.md](verb-forms.md).

## Declension system

There are six productive cases: nominative, accusative, genitive, dative, locative, instrumental. Vocative survives mainly in greetings, kin terms, and expressive forms (`Bože!`, `pane!`, `človeče!`, `priateľu!`) rather than as a full modern case paradigm.

Masculines split into animate and inanimate. In the singular, animate accusative usually equals genitive (`vidím pána`), inanimate accusative usually equals nominative (`vidím stôl`). Animals usually behave like animate in singular but often inanimate in plural. Masculine personal plural has special agreement: `dobrí muži`, `vidím dobrých mužov`; mixed groups take masculine animate.

Feminine common patterns include `žena`, `ulica`, `dlaň`, `kosť`; neuter patterns include `mesto`, `srdce/pole`, `dievča`, `vysvedčenie`. Do not derive a rare noun only from its ending: record its nominative singular, genitive singular, nominative plural, and key irregular form.

| -------------------------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| masculine inanimate `stôl` | `stôl – stola – stolom`         | `stoly – stolov – stolmi`         |
| masculine animate `pán`    | `pán – pána – pánom`            | `páni – pánov – pánmi`            |
| feminine `žena`            | `žena – ženy – ženou`           | `ženy – žien – ženami`            |

## Adjective and participle agreement

Hard adjectives model `pekný`: `pekný, pekná, pekné`; soft adjectives model `cudzí`: `cudzí, cudzia, cudzie`. In singular, common endings include genitive masculine/neuter `-ého`, feminine `-ej`; instrumental `-ým/-ou/-ým`. In plural, masculine animate nominative `-í`, all other nominative/accusative groups normally `-é/-e`; genitive `-ých`; dative `-ým`; locative `-ých`; instrumental `-ými`.

Past and conditional participles agree: `prišiel/prišla/prišlo`, `prišli/prišli/prišli`; distinguish masculine animate plural `boli pripravení` from non-masculine/objects `boli pripravené`. Passive/adjectival participles inflect like adjectives: `napísaný list`, `napísaná správa`.

## Pronouns and determiners

`ten, tá, to` decline and agree; plurals `tí` for masculine animate, `tie` otherwise. Interrogatives: `kto` (who), `čo` (what), `ktorý` (which), `aký` (what kind), `čí` (whose), `koľko` (how many/much). Indefinites/negatives: `niekto/nikto`, `niečo/nič`, `niekde/nikde`, `niekam/nikam`, `každý`, `všetok/všetci`.

`sa` has no nominative and is a reflexive accusative particle; `si` is a reflexive dative. Possessives `môj, tvoj, náš, váš, svoj` agree with the possession; `jeho, jej, ich` remain unchanged. Relative `ktorý` agrees and declines; use `čo` for many neuter/general antecedents.

## Numerals and quantity

`jeden` behaves like an adjective. `dva/dve`, `tri`, `štyri` take plural-like noun forms; `päť` and higher usually take genitive plural. Quantified noun phrases have complex agreement: in neutral formal use, a verb with a 5+ subject is commonly singular neuter (`Päť ľudí prišlo`), though context and alternatives occur. Ordinals inflect as adjectives: `prvý, druhý, tretí`; dates use ordinal/genitive patterns: `piateho mája`.

Collectives and measure words require lexical learning: `dvoje dvere`, `troje nohavice`, `pár dní`, `veľa ľudí`, `málo času`, `niekoľko kníh`.

## Verb morphology

Present classes include `-á-` (`volám`), `-í-` (`robím`), `-uje-` (`kupujem`), `-ne-` (`zabudnem`), and irregular/consonant-alternating types. The infinitive alone is not always sufficient to predict every present form; provide 1st singular, 3rd plural, past masculine/feminine, and aspect partner for a high-frequency verb.

Past: l-participle + clitic forms of `byť`, with no third-person auxiliary: `písal som`, `písali sme`, `písal`. Future: `budem + infinitive` for most imperfectives; perfective present forms normally express future. Conditional: l-participle + `by`; past conditional adds past `byť`: `bol by som prišiel` (formal/literary, use cautiously). Imperatives have lexical stem forms; 1pl/2pl add `-me/-te` to the imperative base.

## Non-finite forms, passive, and impersonal constructions

The present active participle (`pracujúci`) and past active participle (`bývalý`, lexicalized forms) function adjectivally. Passive participles commonly end `-ný/-tý`: `napísaný, urobený, otvorený`. Passive can be `byť + passive participle` (state/process depending context) or reflexive impersonal/passive: `Tu sa predáva chlieb.` Use agentless forms carefully; Slovak often prefers an active indefinite subject over an English passive.

Verbal nouns/derivations are common: `čítať → čítanie`, `rozhodnúť → rozhodnutie`; they decline as nouns. Some are lexicalized; verify stress/quantity and valency.

## Word formation

Prefixes can alter aspect and lexical meaning: `písať → napísať, prepísať, podpísať`; `ísť → prísť, odísť, vojsť, vyjsť`. Suffixes form professions/people (`-ár, -árka, -iteľ, -ka`), abstract nouns (`-osť, -stvo, -nie/-anie`), adjectives (`-ský, -ový, -ný`), and diminutives (`-ček, -ka, -ička`). Do not assume mechanical gender-neutral or feminine forms; use the established word in the domain and respect style preferences.
