# Discourse and Verb Precision

Use this reference when Slovak needs to move beyond correct individual sentences: expressing stance, linking claims, choosing `sa/si`, distinguishing a possessive adjective from genitive possession, and controlling repeated-motion verbs.

## 1. Particles: small words that change the message

Particles do not normally carry a sentence role such as subject or object. They qualify the speaker’s stance, scope, emphasis, or expectation. Choose them for a concrete communicative purpose, not as fillers.

| Purpose                 | Particle(s)          | Meaning in use     | Example                                     |
| ----------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| restriction             | `iba`, `len`         | only               | `Dnes prišla iba Zuzana.`                   |
| minimum / concession    | `aspoň`              | at least           | `Príď aspoň na chvíľu.`                     |
| addition / emphasis     | `aj`, `tiež`         | also, too          | `Prišli aj rodičia.` `Rodičia prišli tiež.` |
| total negation          | `vôbec`              | at all             | `Vôbec tomu nerozumiem.`                    |
| confirmation            | `naozaj`, `skutočne` | really, indeed     | `Naozaj nepríde.`                           |
| certainty               | `určite`             | certainly          | `Určite sa ozvem.`                          |
| probability             | `možno`, `asi`       | perhaps / probably | `Možno bude meškať.` `Asi už spí.`          |
| near limit              | `takmer`, `skoro`    | almost             | `Takmer som zmeškal vlak.`                  |
| completion / impatience | `konečne`            | finally            | `Konečne si tu!`                            |
| side remark             | `mimochodom`         | by the way         | `Mimochodom, už si jej písal/a?`            |
| shared expectation      | `však`               | right?, after all  | `Bolo to pekné, však?`                      |

Position changes focus:

```text
Aj Zuzanini rodičia prišli.      Zuzana’s parents came too (addition focused on subject).
Zuzanini rodičia tiež prišli.    They came too (addition follows prior information).
Iba Peter odpovedal.             Only Peter answered.
Peter odpovedal iba včera.       Peter answered only yesterday.
```

Do not stack particles just because English has several intensifiers. `veľmi naozaj určite` normally sounds uncontrolled. Let one particle do one job.

## 2. Coordination: join ideas by their real relationship

### Addition and accumulation

```text
Jana a Peter prišli.
Prišla aj Jana. / Prišla Jana aj Peter.
Neprišla ani Jana, ani Peter.
Nielen pracuje, ale aj študuje.
Nielenže pracuje, ale ešte aj študuje.
```

Use `a` for neutral addition. Use `aj` when the second element is noteworthy. Use `ani` in negative clauses; do not translate English “either” mechanically.

### Contrast and correction

| Connector                     | Typical force                      | Example                                    |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| `ale`, `no`                   | basic contrast                     | `Chcel prísť, ale nemal čas.`              |
| `lenže`, `ibaže`              | “except that / but the problem is” | `Sľúbil to, lenže neprišiel.`              |
| `avšak`                       | formal written contrast            | `Návrh je zaujímavý, avšak drahý.`         |
| `predsa však`, `napriek tomu` | contrast despite previous fact     | `Bolo chladno; napriek tomu sme išli von.` |
| `síce ... ale`                | concessive balance                 | `Síce prší, ale pôjdeme.`                  |

`avšak` and `napriek tomu` suit formal prose more than casual conversation. `síce` normally anticipates an `ale/lenže` clause.

### Choice, strengthening, and explanation

```text
Môžeme ísť vlakom alebo autobusom.          simple alternative
Buď zavolaj, alebo pošli e-mail.             exclusive framed choice
Prípadne / eventuálne sa stretneme online.  additional possible option
Nielen je lacný, ale dokonca aj spoľahlivý.  strengthening
Navyše má dlhšiu záruku.                     add an important point
Zostal doma, lebo bol chorý.                 reason
Bol chorý, preto zostal doma.                result
```

`respektíve` corrects or narrows a formulation, not merely adds a choice: `Stretneme sa v stredu, respektíve vo štvrtok, ak bude treba.` Use it sparingly.

## 3. Clauses: cause, purpose, time, condition, concession

### Cause and consequence

| Pattern                           | Use                                       | Example                                |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| `lebo`, `pretože`, `keďže`        | subordinate cause                         | `Nešiel, pretože bol chorý.`           |
| `preto, lebo`                     | emphatic reason after result              | `Odišiel preto, lebo nemal čas.`       |
| `preto`, `takže`                  | result                                    | `Bol chorý, preto zostal doma.`        |
| `totiž`, `čiže`, `to jest`, `veď` | explanation/clarification between clauses | `Nemohol prísť, mal totiž veľa práce.` |

`totiž` usually stands after the first word or phrase of its clause (`mal totiž`). `lebo` is conversational-neutral; `pretože` and `keďže` are comfortable in formal writing. `čiže` means “that is / therefore” and should clarify a conclusion, not introduce a random new point.

### Purpose

Use `aby` with a finite verb when the subject, modality, or tense needs to be expressed.

```text
Zavolal som, aby som si overil termín.
Vysvetlili to tak, aby tomu všetci rozumeli.
Výskum sa realizuje s cieľom, aby bolo možné hypotézu potvrdiť.
```

Useful formal anchors: `preto`, `na to`, `s tým cieľom`, `len preto`, `iba preto`. Avoid _`pre to aby`_ as an English-style “for to”; write `preto, aby...` only when it actually means “for that reason, so that...”.

### Time

| Link                          | Meaning                  | Example                                    |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| `keď`                         | when                     | `Keď prišiel, začali sme.`                 |
| `kým`                         | while / until            | `Počkám, kým prídeš.`                      |
| `hneď ako`, `len čo`, `sotva` | as soon as / hardly when | `Hneď ako prídem, zavolám.`                |
| `skôr než`, `predtým než`     | before                   | `Odišiel skôr, než sme začali.`            |
| `odvtedy, čo`                 | since                    | `Odvtedy, čo sa presťahoval, sa nevidíme.` |
| `až keď`, `iba keď`           | only when                | `Pochopil to, až keď to skúsil.`           |

Pair time expressions deliberately: `odvtedy, čo ...`, `dovtedy, kým ...`, `vtedy, keď ...`, `teraz, keď ...`. The comma goes between the pointer word and the conjunction.

### Conditions and concession

```text
Ak bude pršať, zostaneme doma.                 if: open future condition
Len ak bude pršať, zostaneme doma.              only if
V prípade, že bude pršať, ...                   formal “in the event that”
Za predpokladu, že sú údaje správne, ...        stated assumption
Keby som mal čas, prišiel by som.               hypothetical condition
Aj keby pršalo, pôjdeme.                        concession: even if
Hoci / aj keď je riešenie drahé, je účinné.     concession: although
```

Avoid `v prípade, že` in ordinary small talk where `ak` is cleaner. `ak` is the most neutral “if”; conditional `pokiaľ` is also standard (`Pokiaľ sa káva pije s mierou, neškodí.`), and `pokiaľ` further means “as far as / as long as” (`pokiaľ viem`, `pokiaľ ide o`).

## 4. `sa` and `si`: choose the category before the form

### Inseparable reflexive verbs

Some verbs simply require `sa` or `si`. Removing it creates a different verb or no valid intended meaning.

```text
starať sa o + acc.        starať sa o domácnosť
báť sa + gen.             báť sa tmy
usilovať sa o + acc.      usilovať sa o úspech
uchádzať sa o + acc.      uchádzať sa o prácu
všímať si + acc.          všímať si detaily
všimnúť si + acc.         všimnúť si chybu
darí sa mi                I am doing well / it is going well for me
podobať sa na + acc.      podobať sa na otca
správať sa                behave
diať sa                   happen
```

Learn their whole case frame. `Bojím sa psa`, not _`bojím psa`_; `uchádzam sa o miesto`, not _`uchádzam miesto`_.

### Reaction reflexives

Adding `sa` may recast an action as the subject’s reaction to someone/something else.

```text
Mama urazila Johannu.              Mother offended Johanna.
Johanna sa urazila na mamu.        Johanna took offence at Mother.
Robert zobudil dieťa.              Robert woke the child.
Dieťa sa zobudilo.                 The child woke up.
Firma zamestnala Janu.             The company employed Jana.
Jana sa zamestnala.                Jana got a job.
```

Do not assume each pair maps to English “self.” Check who is acting and who undergoes the change.

### Reciprocal `sa/si`

Use `sa/si` for mutual action when the verb permits it. Add `navzájom` only if clarity or emphasis is needed.

```text
Poznáme sa.                        We know each other.
Písali si.                         They wrote to each other.
Pomáhali si navzájom.              They helped one another.
```

### Meaning changes with `sa/si`

Some pairs are lexical, not predictable:

```text
predstaviť niekoho niekomu         introduce someone to someone
predstaviť si niečo                imagine something
spomenúť niečo                     mention something
spomenúť si na + acc.              remember something
vážiť niečo                        weigh something
vážiť si niekoho                   value/respect someone
```

Treat the added particle and complement as part of the new dictionary entry.

## 5. Possession: adjective or genitive phrase?

Choose the construction by how specific, short, and human the owner is.

| Form                 | Best use                                                | Example                                                              |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| possessive adjective | one named animate owner; compact label                  | `manželova dovolenka`, `Kukučínova ulica`                            |
| possessive genitive  | owner needs detail, plural, non-human, or general class | `dovolenka môjho manžela`, `kancelária prezidenta`, `názory občanov` |

The contrast can change meaning:

```text
Manažérova dovolenka nebola pekná.   One particular manager’s holiday.
Dovolenka manažéra je vždy krátka.   A manager’s holiday, as a typical/general category.
```

Use a possessive adjective only when the owner can be expressed as one animate noun in singular. If the owner is expanded (`môjho bývalého spolužiaka`, `minister školstva`, `bratia Grimmovci`), genitive is normally clearer.

## 6. Deverbals: activity vs. completed result

Deverbal nouns turn events into things, concepts, headings, or administrative objects. Their aspect often preserves the source verb’s viewpoint.

| Imperfective process                        | Perfective result/event                       |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| `poskytovanie služieb` (providing services) | `poskytnutie služby` (provision of a service) |
| `objednávanie` (ordering process)           | `objednanie` (placing/completing an order)    |
| `liečenie` (treatment)                      | `vyliečenie` (cure)                           |
| `nosenie` (carrying)                        | `prinesenie` (bringing)                       |
| `myslenie` (thinking)                       | `rozhodnutie` (decision)                      |

High-frequency endings include `-anie`, `-enie`, `-tie`, and `-nutie`. Use a process noun for duration/repetition; use the perfective-derived noun for a bounded result. Avoid nominalizing every plain verb: `Rozhodli sme sa` is often clearer than `Uskutočnili sme rozhodnutie`.

## 7. Movement, repetition, and frequency

Slovak distinguishes a single running event, repeated movement in a period, and a recurring habit.

```text
Bežím na autobus.                  one current/single running event
Deti behajú celý deň po záhrade.   repeated/back-and-forth activity in a period
Keď bol mladý, behával v parku.    regular old habit
```

| Verb       | Core use                                                  | Common extended use                     |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| `bežať`    | run once / be running                                     | `bežať na autobus`; `čas beží`          |
| `behať`    | repeated/various-direction movement, intentional exercise | `behať po úradoch`; `behať pre zdravie` |
| `behávať`  | customary recurring activity                              | `behávať každé ráno`                    |
| `niesť`    | carry one item/event                                      | `niesť kufor`                           |
| `nosiť`    | carry repeatedly / wear                                   | `nosiť noviny`; `nosiť kabát`           |
| `nosievať` | habitual repeated carrying/wearing                        | `nosievať to do práce`                  |

The pattern recurs across motion verbs, but it is lexical rather than fully mechanical. Teach a whole contrast with time adverbs: `teraz`, `celý deň`, `zvyčajne`, `kedysi`.

## 8. Verb-prefix families: use semantic scenes

Prefixes are not a generic “perfective marker.” They add a spatial, resultative, distributive, or lexical meaning. Learn them through a scene and check the new verb’s case frame.

| Family        | Productive scene                   | Examples                                                                                                          |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `bežať/behať` | direction/extent of running        | `pribežať` arrive running; `odbehnúť` run away; `zabehnúť` run in/call in; `prebehnúť` run across                 |
| `niesť/nosiť` | source and destination of carrying | `priniesť` bring; `odniesť` take away; `vyniesť` carry out/up; `preniesť` transfer/carry across                   |
| `platiť`      | payment relationship               | `zaplatiť` pay; `preplatiť` overpay/reimburse depending context; `doplatiť` pay the remainder; `vyplatiť` pay out |
| `tvoriť`      | creation/change of composition     | `vytvoriť` create; `dotvoriť` finish shaping; `pretvoriť` transform; `stvoriť` create (often elevated)            |
| `myslieť`     | directed thought/result            | `pomyslieť si` think of; `premyslieť` think through; `vymyslieť` invent/think up; `zamyslieť sa` reflect          |

Do not promise an aspect partner merely from a prefix. `preplatiť`, for example, has more than one real-world sense. Verify its object and context before translating.

## 9. Writing forms: make style visible

### Brief report (`správa`)

A report is factual, concise, current, and objectively organized. Lead with what happened, then relevant time/place/participants, then confirmed consequence. Attribute uncertainty: `podľa dostupných informácií`, `polícia uviedla`, `zatiaľ nie je známe`.

### Description (`opis`)

Order stable details: overall impression → position/appearance → distinguishing features → relevant function. Use specific adjective-noun agreement and avoid a list with no viewpoint.

### Review (`recenzia`)

Name the work and context; summarize only enough to orient; evaluate with criteria and evidence; recommend for a defined audience. Prefer `zaujalo ma`, `presvedčivo pôsobí`, `slabšou stránkou je`, `odporučil/a by som` over unsupported `je to super`.

### Opinion essay / public argument (`úvaha`)

State question or claim, develop reasons, include a counterpoint, then qualify the conclusion. Connect every paragraph explicitly. Do not confuse a personal judgment with a verified fact.

## Final control pass

1. Does each connector express addition, contrast, cause, result, time, condition, or concession precisely?
2. Is `sa/si` part of the verb’s lexical frame, a reciprocal, a reaction, or an impersonal/passive construction?
3. Does the possession construction match the owner’s specificity and complexity?
4. Does a deverbal noun need the process or completed-result aspect?
5. Does a prefix or frequency verb carry the intended spatial/habitual meaning?
