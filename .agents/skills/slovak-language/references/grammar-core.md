# Slovak Grammar Core

## Nouns, gender, and the six cases

Slovak has no articles. Learn every noun with its gender and a frame, not as an isolated English gloss. Main genders: masculine animate (`muž`), masculine inanimate (`stôl`), feminine (`žena`), neuter (`mesto`). Masculine animate singular accusative normally equals genitive; masculine inanimate singular accusative normally equals nominative.

| Case         | Main question/function                                | Core frames                              |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Nominative   | kto? čo? subject/name                                 | `To je nový stôl.`                       |
| Accusative   | koho? čo? direct object; destination after many preps | `Vidím nového suseda.` `Idem na poštu.`  |
| Genitive     | koho? čoho? possession, absence, origin, quantity     | `bez cukru`, `z Bratislavy`, `niet času` |
| Dative       | komu? čomu? recipient/beneficiary                     | `Pomáham bratovi.`                       |
| Locative     | o kom? o čom? location/topic; only with prepositions  | `v meste`, `o práci`, `na stole`         |
| Instrumental | s kým? s čím? accompaniment/means                     | `s kamarátom`, `autobusom`               |

High-frequency singular models:

| Type                | nom.  | acc.  | gen.  | dat.   | loc.   | instr. |
| ------------------- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ------ | ------ | ------ |
| masculine inanimate | stôl  | stôl  | stola | stolu  | stole  | stolom |
| masculine animate   | muž   | muža  | muža  | mužovi | mužovi | mužom  |
| feminine `žena`     | žena  | ženu  | ženy  | žene   | žene   | ženou  |
| feminine `ulica`    | ulica | ulicu | ulice | ulici  | ulici  | ulicou |
| neuter `mesto`      | mesto | mesto | mesta | mestu  | meste  | mestom |
| neuter `pole`       | pole  | pole  | poľa  | poľu   | poli   | poľom  |

Plural is less predictable. Teach a noun's plural separately, especially masculine animate (`muži`, `ľudia`, `priatelia`), neuter (`mestá`, `polia`, `dievčatá`), and genitive plural (`bez mužov, miest, žien`). Numerals require special attention: `jeden muž`, `dva muži`, `dve ženy/mestá`, but `päť mužov/žien/miest`.

## Adjectives, possessives, and pronouns

Adjectives agree with the noun in gender, number, and case: `nový dom`, `nová kniha`, `nové mesto`; `s novým domom`, `s novou knihou`, `s novým mestom`. Long adjective forms are normal; use short forms only in fixed or lexicalized uses.

Possessives: `môj, tvoj, náš, váš` agree with the possessed item (`moja sestra`, `moje auto`); `jeho, jej, ich` do not decline. `svoj` means one's own and normally replaces another possessive when the owner is the subject: `Peter si zobral svoj kabát.`

Personal pronouns are often omitted because the verb ending identifies the subject. Use them for contrast/emphasis: `Ja pôjdem, ty zostaň.` Key forms: `mňa/ma, mne/mi, mnou`; `teba/ťa, tebe/ti, tebou`; `ho/jeho, mu/jemu, ním`; `ju/jej, jej, ňou`; `nás, nám, nami`; `vás, vám, vami`; `ich, im, nimi`.

Formal `Vy` takes plural verb forms: `Ako sa máte? Ste pripravený?` Address an individual formally with capitalized `Vy, Vám, Váš` in letters and email.

## Verbs and time

The infinitive usually ends in `-ť`. Present endings vary by class; introduce frequent verbs as full patterns:

| Infinitive | 1sg     | 2sg     | 3sg    | 1pl      | 2pl      | 3pl    |
| ---------- | ------- | ------- | ------ | -------- | -------- | ------ |
| robiť      | robím   | robíš   | robí   | robíme   | robíte   | robia  |
| čítať      | čítam   | čítaš   | číta   | čítame   | čítate   | čítajú |
| bývať      | bývam   | bývaš   | býva   | bývame   | bývate   | bývajú |
| kupovať    | kupujem | kupuješ | kupuje | kupujeme | kupujete | kupujú |
| ísť        | idem    | ideš    | ide    | ideme    | idete    | idú    |
| chcieť     | chcem   | chceš   | chce   | chceme   | chcete   | chcú   |
| môcť       | môžem   | môžeš   | môže   | môžeme   | môžete   | môžu   |
| vedieť     | viem    | vieš    | vie    | vieme    | viete    | vedia  |
| byť        | som     | si      | je     | sme      | ste      | sú     |
| mať        | mám     | máš     | má     | máme     | máte     | majú   |

Past tense = `-l` participle agreeing with the subject + present `byť` clitic where needed: `bol som`, `bola som`, `boli sme`; `urobil som to`. In third person, `byť` is omitted: `Včera prišiel.`

Future: imperfective verbs commonly use `budem + infinitive` (`budem pracovať`); perfective verbs use present-looking forms with future meaning (`napíšem list`, `prídem zajtra`). `ísť` has `pôjdem, pôjdeš...`.

Conditional: past participle + `by` clitic: `chcel by som / chcela by som`, `mohli by ste`. Imperative: learn each frequent verb's form: `rob!`, `robte!`, `poď!`, `poďte!`, `napíš!`, `napíšte!`. Negate with `ne-`: `nechoďte`, `nepíšte`.

## Aspect and motion

Most Slovak verbs belong to an imperfective/perfective pair. Imperfective describes process, repeated/habitual action, or an unbounded activity; perfective frames an event as completed, achieved, or one-time. `Písal list.` = he was writing (or spent time writing) a letter; `Napísal list.` = he finished writing it. Do not force a pair where lexical usage differs; learn common pairs in sentences.

Prefixes often create perfectives: `písať → napísať`, `čítať → prečítať`, `robiť → urobiť`. Other pairs differ by suffix (`kupovať/kúpiť`, `dávať/dať`, `otvárať/otvoriť`) or use a different stem (`hovoriť/povedať`, `brať/vziať`, `klásť/položiť`). A prefix can also change the meaning (`písať → podpísať` 'sign'). Do not translate English tense mechanically. Full treatment: [aspect-and-motion.md](aspect-and-motion.md).

Motion needs distinction: `ísť` (one directed trip, on foot or by vehicle: `idem pešo`, `idem autom`) vs. `chodiť` (habitual, repeated, or round trips: `chodím do práce`). Directional pairs: `prísť/prichádzať`, `odísť/odchádzať`, `vstúpiť/vstupovať`, `vyjsť/vychádzať`.

## Prepositions and space

Memorize the case with the preposition. Central contrast: location = locative/instrumental; destination/motion toward = accusative for changing prepositions.

| Pattern         | Meaning                  | Example                           |
| --------------- | ------------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| `v/vo + loc.`   | in                       | `v škole`, `v januári`            |
| `do + gen.`     | into/to                  | `do školy`, `do Bratislavy`       |
| `na + loc.`     | on/at                    | `na stole`, `na Slovensku`        |
| `na + acc.`     | onto; for a purpose/time | `na stôl`, `na obed`, `na týždeň` |
| `z/zo + gen.`   | out of/from              | `z domu`, `zo školy`              |
| `k/ku + dat.`   | to/toward                | `k lekárovi`, `ku mne`            |
| `s/so + instr.` | with                     | `s priateľmi`                     |
| `o + loc.`      | about; at a clock time   | `o filme`, `o tretej`             |
| `pre + acc.`    | for/because of           | `pre teba`, `pre dážď`            |
| `bez + gen.`    | without                  | `bez problémov`                   |
| `u + gen.`      | at someone's place       | `u lekára`, `u mamy`              |

`pred, za, nad, pod, medzi` take instrumental for static location (`pred domom`) and accusative for movement toward/into it (`pred dom`). `po + loc.` commonly means after/around/along; `po + acc.` in `ísť po chlieb` means go to fetch.

## Sentence structure, negation, and clitics

Word order is flexible; information structure drives it. New/focused information tends later: `Knihu číta Jana.` Use neutral SVO only as a default. Questions often use intonation or a question word: `Prídeš?`, `Kedy prídeš?`; yes/no `či` belongs in embedded clauses.

Negation is `ne-` attached to verb: `neviem, nemám, nepôjdem`. Negative pronouns co-occur with verb negation: `Nikto neprišiel.`, `Nič nevidím.` Genitive after negation survives mainly in fixed or bookish phrases (`niet času`, `nebolo miesta`); everyday Slovak uses the accusative: `Nemám čas.`

Short clitics (`som, si, sme, ste, by, mi, ti, sa, si, ho, mu`) occupy the second position of their clause, after its first stressed constituent: `Včera som tam bol.` `Peter mi to dal.` Avoid placing them mechanically at sentence start.

Reflexive `sa` and dative reflexive `si` are not interchangeable: `učím sa` (I am learning), `kúpim si kávu` (I buy myself a coffee). Learn with the verb.

## Phonology-driven spelling

Stress is normally on the first syllable. Vowel length and softness affect spelling and forms. `i/í` and `y/ý` sound alike in standard pronunciation but encode spelling rules. After `d, t, n, l`, `e, i, í` generally signal softness (`deti, ticho, nikdy`); `ď, ť, ň, ľ` mark soft consonants where following vowels do not show it. The rhythmic law generally avoids two consecutive long syllables in native words: `krásny`, not *`krásný`; `bývam`, not *`bývám`.

Use `ch` as a separate alphabetic letter after `h`. Keep diacritics exact: `č, š, ž, ď, ť, ň, ľ, ŕ, ĺ, á, é, í, ó, ú, ý, ä, ô`.
