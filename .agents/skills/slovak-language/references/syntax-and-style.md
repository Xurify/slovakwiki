# Syntax, Style, and Register

## Clause structure and information order

Slovak permits flexible constituent order because morphology signals roles. Use order to manage topic, focus, contrast, and rhythm. Neutral patterns often resemble SVO (`Jana číta knihu.`), but `Knihu číta Jana.` naturally focuses the subject. End position is commonly prominent/new information. Do not preserve English order if case endings make a more natural Slovak order available.

Subject pronouns are usually omitted with finite verbs; include them for contrast, clarity, or politeness. Existential sentences frequently use `je/sú`, `je tu`, `niet + genitive`: `Na stole je kniha.` `Niet dôvodu na paniku.`

## Clitic order

Unstressed clitics occupy second position in a clause after its first stressed constituent, not necessarily after the first word: `Včera som mu to povedal.` `Tá kniha sa mi páči.` Typical cluster order is conditional/auxiliary, reflexive, short dative, short accusative, but use idiomatic established patterns rather than forcing an abstract sequence. Avoid clause-initial `som/si/by/sa` in ordinary declaratives.

## Negation and polarity

Prefix finite verbs with `ne-`: `neviem, nebudem, neprišiel`. Negative pronouns normally require verbal negation: `Nikto nevolal.` `Nikdy tam nechodím.` `Nič som nevidel.` Double negation is standard, not emphatic. With `ani`, use negation: `Nevidel ani jedného človeka.`

## Coordination and subordination

Core conjunctions: `a` (and), `ale` (but), `alebo` (or), `pretože/lebo` (because), `preto/takže` (therefore/so), `ak` (if), `keby` (if hypothetical), `keď` (when/because), `kým` (while/until), `hoci/aj keď` (although), `aby` (so that/in order that), `že` (that), `či` (whether). Subordinate-clause word order stays Slovak, not English inversion: `Neviem, či príde.`

Use relative `ktorý` for a specific agreeing antecedent: `muž, ktorého poznám`; use `čo` for broad/neuter antecedents and clauses: `To, čo hovoríš...`. Punctuate dependent clauses with commas in standard prose.

## Direct and reported speech

Direct speech commonly uses a colon/dash or quotation style appropriate to the document. Indirect speech often uses `že` or `či`: `Povedal, že príde.` Tense does not require automatic English-style backshift; choose time relative to the reporting context. Requests use `aby`: `Požiadal ma, aby som prišiel.`

## Comparison, modality, and impersonal expressions

Comparatives are usually `-ší/-ejší` or `viac/menej + adjective/adverb`; the comparison complement is `ako` or `než`: `starší ako ja`, `viac než päť`. Superlative takes `naj-`: `najlepší`. Express obligation/possibility with `musieť`, `môcť`, `treba`, `možno`, `dá sa`: `Treba to urobiť.` `Dá sa tam dostať vlakom.`

Experiencer constructions use dative: `Je mi zima.`, `Je mi ľúto.`, `Chýba mi čas.`, `Páči sa mi to.` Body sensations often accusative: `Bolí ma hlava.`

## Editing for register

| Register              | Prefer                                                                                     |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| conversation          | short clauses, frequent words, `Ahoj`, contractions/ellipsis only where natural            |
| neutral professional  | `Dobrý deň`, direct request and clear deadline, respectful `vykanie`                       |
| formal administrative | complete sentences, explicit referents, standard terms, `Vážený...`, `S pozdravom/S úctou` |
| academic/public       | defined terms, qualified claims, connective logic, source attribution                      |
| literary/creative     | preserve voice; explain nonstandard choice only when asked                                 |

Avoid false formality: an inflated calque can sound less professional than concise standard Slovak. Avoid overusing `byť`, passive, and abstract nouns when a direct verb is clearer.

## Czech contact and regional variation

Slovak and Czech are closely related, but do not silently replace a Slovak standard word/form with its Czech counterpart. Regional dialects and colloquial varieties are real; standard Slovak is the default unless the user asks for a dialect or authentic spoken tone. Flag cross-border false friends and nonstandard forms only if they affect comprehension, correctness, or target register.
