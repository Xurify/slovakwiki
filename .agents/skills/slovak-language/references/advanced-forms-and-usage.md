# Advanced Forms and Usage

Use this reference for forms that often decide whether Slovak sounds genuinely competent: impersonal clauses, derived verb forms, irregular noun classes, number expressions, and formal correspondence.

## 1. “All”, “same”, and quantity words

### `všetok`: select the form by meaning and agreement

`všetok` declines like an adjective. In ordinary speech, its plural and neuter singular forms do most of the work.

| Meaning                                | Slovak                 | Example                                      |
| -------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| all people / masculine personal plural | `všetci`               | `Všetci hostia už prišli.`                   |
| all people, object                     | `všetkých`             | `Pozvali všetkých hostí.`                    |
| all other plurals, animals included    | `všetky`               | `Všetky izby sú obsadené.`                   |
| everything                             | `všetko`               | `Všetko som pochopil/a.`                     |
| all of one mass/whole noun             | `všetok/všetka/všetko` | `Zjedol všetok šalát.` `Vypila všetku vodu.` |

Do not use `všetko` before a count plural noun: say `všetky knihy`, not _`všetko knihy`_. It can itself decline: `o všetkom`, `so všetkými`, `vo všetkých prípadoch`.

### Three meanings of “the same”

| Form                           | Meaning                                         | Example                                          |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| `ten istý / tá istá / to isté` | the identical person or thing                   | `Bol to ten istý človek.`                        |
| `taký istý`                    | the same kind; “just as …”                      | `Má rovnaký názor? — Áno, je taký istý ako môj.` |
| `rovnaký`                      | same/equal/comparable; often general comparison | `Máme rovnaké podmienky.`                        |

`ten istý` needs the demonstrative. `taký istý` normally invites an `ako` comparison. Do not use English-style _`same as me`_ without agreement: `rovnaký ako ja`, `rovnaké ako moje`.

### Useful non-exact quantities

| Form                       | Typical government             | Example                                 |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| `veľa`, `málo`, `trochu`   | usually genitive               | `veľa ľudí`, `málo času`, `trochu vody` |
| `niekoľko`                 | genitive plural                | `niekoľko otázok`                       |
| `pár`, `zopár`             | genitive plural in neutral use | `pár dní`, `zopár nápadov`              |
| `každý`, `celý`            | agree as adjectives            | `každý deň`, `celú noc`                 |
| `oba/obidva`, `obe/obidve` | “both”, agreement matters      | `obaja kolegovia`, `obe možnosti`       |

`obaja/obidvaja` is for two male people; `obe/obidve` for feminine or neuter groups; `oba/obidva` commonly for masculine inanimate. Prefer one family consistently in a short text (`oba` or `obidva`).

## 2. Passive, reflexive passive, and agentless Slovak

English passive is not a default Slovak template. First decide what the sentence should foreground.

| Focus                                        | Preferred pattern                | Example                           |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| known agent                                  | active, flexible order           | `List napísala Viera.`            |
| result/state/history                         | `byť + passive participle`       | `Most bol postavený v roku 1930.` |
| general process, seller/operator unimportant | `sa` construction                | `Lístky sa predávajú online.`     |
| unnamed people in a one-off event            | active plural / indefinite agent | `Včera mi volali z banky.`        |

Passive participles typically use `-ný/-ený/-ovaný/-tý`:

```text
zatvoriť → zatvorený       napísať → napísaný
prekvapiť → prekvapený     organizovať → organizovaný
zabudnúť → zabudnutý       vyrobiť → vyrobený
```

They behave like adjectives: `zatvorené okno`, `zatvorená predajňa`, `zatvorený obchod`. Check whether a state adjective is more natural than a true passive: `Obchod je otvorený` means “the shop is open.”

### `sa` constructions: do not confuse the jobs of `sa`

`sa` can mean several different things:

```text
Učím sa po slovensky.            lexical reflexive: I am learning
Stretli sa po práci.             reciprocal: they met each other
Tu sa predáva chlieb.            general/reflexive passive: bread is sold here
Ako sa to píše?                  conventional agentless question: how is it written?
Polievka sa začína variť.        process/change of state: the soup is beginning to boil
```

Do not insert a specific agent into the reflexive passive. If who did it matters, choose active: `Zmluvu podpísal riaditeľ`, not a forced _`Zmluva sa podpísala riaditeľom`_.

## 3. Verbal adjectives, verbal nouns, and active participles

### Passive/adjectival participles (“written”, “closed”)

Use these before a noun or with `byť`:

```text
napísaný list                 list bol napísaný včera
zabudnuté heslo               heslo bolo zabudnuté
organizované podujatie        podujatie bolo organizované mestom
```

The aspect source matters. A perfective base usually suits a completed result (`napísaný list`); an imperfective base may name an ongoing/repeated process when idiomatic. Learn established forms with their verbs rather than generate rare ones blindly.

### Verbal nouns (“reading”, “decision”, “opening”)

Common endings are `-nie/-tie`.

| Verb               | Noun                   | Typical sense                |
| ------------------ | ---------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| `čítať / prečítať` | `čítanie / prečítanie` | activity / completed reading |
| `otvoriť`          | `otvorenie`            | opening                      |
| `organizovať`      | `organizovanie`        | organising                   |
| `stretnúť sa`      | `stretnutie`           | meeting                      |
| `rozhodnúť`        | `rozhodnutie`          | decision                     |

Keep aspect when it changes the event boundary: `čítanie knihy` is the activity; `prečítanie knihy` is completing it. A verbal noun declines and often keeps the source verb’s complement: `počas čítania knihy`, `po rozhodnutí vlády`.

### Present active participles (“a person travelling”, “a surprising result”)

These usually end in `-úci/-iaci` and agree like adjectives.

```text
cestujúci vlak       travelling/moving train; also “passenger” as a noun
píšuce dieťa         a child writing
žena sediaca pri okne
prekvapujúci výsledok
vedúca úloha          leading role
```

They are productive for clear, current actions but can sound bureaucratic if overused. In conversation, a relative clause is often easier: `žena, ktorá sedí pri okne`.

Short written forms `-úc/-iac` can attach an accompanying action:

```text
Sediac v záhrade, pili čaj.       While sitting in the garden, they drank tea.
Pijúc čaj, počúvali rádio.        While drinking tea, they listened to the radio.
Vypijúc čaj, odišiel domov.       Having drunk the tea, he went home.
```

These are literary/formal; avoid them in a beginner conversation or plain business email.

## 4. Habit, repetition, and “used to”

Frequentative verbs in `-ávať/-ievať` add a nuance of habitual, occasional, or repeated action. They are not required for every habit, but they are worth recognizing and using where the repetition itself matters.

```text
jesť → jedávať             Kde zvyčajne jedávaš?
robiť → robievať           Robieva podobné chyby.
chodiť → chodievať         Chodievame tam v lete.
vidieť → vídavať           Vídavam ho v centre.
mať → mávať                V pondelok mávajú zatvorené.
```

In the past they commonly mean “used to”: `Chodieval do kostola.` For a simple present routine, ordinary imperfective is usually enough: `Každý deň chodím do práce.`

## 5. Impersonal predicates and absence

### `niet + genitive`

`niet` is a compact present-tense “there is not / there are no.” Follow it with genitive.

```text
Niet vody.                    There is no water.
Niet nikoho doma.             Nobody is at home.
Niet dôvodu na paniku.        There is no reason to panic.
Niet sa čomu čudovať.         It is not surprising.
```

For past/future use `nebolo/nebude`: `Nebolo miesta.`, `Nebude času.` The phrase is concise and somewhat elevated in everyday conversation; `nie je` is often plainer.

### `treba`, `vidno/vidieť`, `počuť`

These present-tense predicates commonly omit `je`.

```text
Treba si dávať pozor.         One/you need to be careful.
Bude treba rozhodnúť.         It will be necessary to decide.
Vidno, že prší.               It is apparent that it is raining.
Odtiaľ zle počuť.             One cannot hear well from there.
Počuť, že je unavený.         You can hear that he is tired.
```

`treba` is impersonal: do not force personal agreement (_`trebám`_). Use `musím` when the obligation belongs specifically to the speaker: `Musím odísť.`

## 6. Conditional time and fear/hope clauses

### Real vs. hypothetical vs. past-counterfactual

```text
Ak bude pršať, zostaneme doma.                 real possibility
Keby som mal viac času, učil by som sa viac.   hypothetical present/future
Keby som bol mal čas, bol by som prišiel.      contrary-to-fact past
```

In a past counterfactual, `bol` appears in both the `keby` clause and the result: `Keby bola vedela, bola by zavolala.` In natural modern use, forms vary by region and style, but the pattern is essential for recognition and formal production.

A pluperfect-like `bol` can appear in a past relative clause to mark earlier completion, but it is optional when sequence is already clear: `Košeľu, ktorú si (bol) kúpil včera...`.

### `báť sa`: `že` vs. `aby`

```text
Bojím sa, že mešká.           I fear / suspect that he is late.
Bojím sa, aby som nemeškal.   I am afraid I might be late; I hope not to be.
Dúfam, že neprší.             I hope that it is not raining.
```

After `báť sa, aby`, the following verb is normally negative. This is a high-value contrast: `že` reports the feared proposition; `aby + negation` expresses prevention/worry that it may happen.

## 7. Diminutives: meaning, form, restraint

Diminutives can signal small size, affection, informality, mitigation, or a lexicalized different meaning. They are pervasive, but not automatically “cute”; context decides.

| Base     | Diminutive       | Notes                                    |
| -------- | ---------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| `pohár`  | `pohárik`        | small glass; often a shot/glass of drink |
| `dom`    | `domček`         | little house                             |
| `pes`    | `psík`, `psíček` | little dog; affectionate                 |
| `kniha`  | `knižka`         | booklet/little book                      |
| `ruka`   | `ručička`        | little hand; also clock hand             |
| `okno`   | `okienko`        | little window/service window             |
| `mesto`  | `mestečko`       | small town                               |
| `slovo`  | `slovíčko`       | little word; often a vocabulary item     |
| `pivo`   | `pivko/pivečko`  | friendly/affectionate beer order         |
| `lístok` | from `list`      | leaf; ticket; menu in `jedálny lístok`   |
| `rožok`  | from `roh`       | bread roll, not simply “small corner”    |

Common families are masculine `-ík/-ok/-ček`, feminine `-ka/-(i)čka`, neuter `-ko/-(e)čko`. Treat each frequent form as a word with its own register and meaning. Avoid making a novel diminutive in official or technical prose unless it is established.

## 8. Special neuter people and young-animal nouns

`dievča`, `dieťa`, and young-animal names such as `šteňa` and `mača` use a special neuter pattern. Their endings insert `-ať-/-at-` outside the nominative.

```text
dievča → od dievčaťa → s dievčaťom → dievčatá
dieťa  → od dieťaťa  → s dieťaťom  → deti
šteňa  → šteňatá
mača   → mačatá
```

They take neuter agreement in singular: `malé dievča`, `malé dieťa`. Do not assign feminine agreement to `dievča` based on meaning.

## 9. Vocative remnants

The nominative normally works for direct address: `Jana, poď sem!` `Pán Novák, môžete...?` A small set of male expressive/vocative forms remains common:

```text
pán → pane!              Boh → Bože!
človek → človeče!        chlapec → chlapče!
priateľ → priateľu!
```

Use them only where idiomatic. `pane` is established in service language (`Čo si želáte, pane?`); forms such as `človeče` can sound emotionally marked. Do not invent vocatives for every name or noun.

## 10. Numbers beyond the basic counting rule

### Cases of numerals

Outside simple subject/object positions, put the numeral into the required case; the following noun changes too.

| Case/frame      | 2                  | 3                  | 4                   | 5                    |
| --------------- | ------------------ | ------------------ | ------------------- | -------------------- |
| `do + gen.`     | `dvoch rokov`      | `troch rokov`      | `štyroch rokov`     | `piatich rokov`      |
| `k + dat.`      | `dvom rokom`       | `trom rokom`       | `štyrom rokom`      | `piatim rokom`       |
| `po + loc.`     | `po dvoch rokoch`  | `po troch rokoch`  | `po štyroch rokoch` | `po piatich rokoch`  |
| `pred + instr.` | `pred dvoma rokmi` | `pred troma rokmi` | `pred štyrmi rokmi` | `pred piatimi rokmi` |

With higher numbers, colloquial usage may leave some forms uninflected, but the declined forms are important for careful writing. Learn `pred niekoľkými dňami` and `pred mnohými rokmi` as fixed usable patterns.

### Subject agreement with 5+

For `päť` and higher, neutral formal usage often takes singular neuter predicate: `Päť ľudí prišlo.` Masculine-person agreement can appear when the people are foregrounded: `Prišli ôsmi kamaráti.` Avoid treating this as a simple English plural rule; choose the neutral form unless context motivates the personal one.

### Numbered objects, sets, frequency, fractions, decimals

```text
Idem osmičkou.                    I take the number 8 (tram/bus).
Bývam na štvorke.                 I am staying in room 4.
dvoje nohavice / troje dvere      two pairs of trousers / three doors
dvakrát, trikrát, niekoľkokrát    twice, three times, several times
pol roka, štvrť litra             half a year, a quarter litre
dve tretiny, tri štvrtiny         two thirds, three quarters
jedno percento, dve percentá, päť percent
jedna celá päť                    1.5 (literally “one whole five”)
```

Plural-only nouns (`nohavice`, `dvere`, `okuliare`, `hodinky`) are counted with `dvoje/troje`, not `dva/tri`. For an ordinary pair use `pár`: `pár topánok` 'a pair of shoes'. Use `-krát` for “times,” but `raz` also works: `jeden raz`, `dva razy`.

### Dates, months, and ordinal numbers

Month names are lowercase in Slovak. State a date with an ordinal in the genitive:

```text
Koľkého je dnes? — Je tridsiateho januára.
Prišiel prvého mája.
V Bratislave 10. apríla 2026
```

Use `v januári`, `v marci`, `v máji`, `v auguste`, etc. For complex ordinals: `dvadsiaty prvý`; in a year, only the final segment is ordinal: `tisíc deväťsto šesťdesiaty štvrtý`.

## 11. Correspondence: register is grammatical

| Relationship         | Opening                                       | Closing                   |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| formal               | `Vážený pán Novák!` / `Vážená pani Nováková!` | `S pozdravom` / `S úctou` |
| neutral professional | `Dobrý deň,`                                  | `S pozdravom`             |
| friend/family        | `Milá Eva!` / `Milý Peter!` / `Ahoj, Eva!`    | `Maj sa`, `Pozdravujem`   |

Capitalize `Vy`, `Vám`, `Váš` for one formal addressee in a letter. Use lower case for plural/general audience unless house style requires otherwise.

```text
Ďakujem Vám za Váš list.
Odpovedám na Váš e-mail z 18. júna.
Ďakujem Vám vopred za odpoveď.
Teším sa na Vašu odpoveď.
Ospravedlňujem sa, že som Vám nenapísal/a skôr.
```

Do not pair a formal greeting with `ty` forms, and do not use an exaggerated letter formula for a short practical request. Match the opening, pronouns, verb forms, and closing.

## Production checks

Before sending advanced Slovak, ask:

1. Is an English passive hiding a clearer Slovak active or `sa` construction?
2. Did a derived form preserve the intended aspect and complement?
3. Does `všetok`, `ten istý`, or the numeral agree in gender, case, and meaning?
4. Are a diminutive and a vocative appropriate for the social register?
5. Are dates, numbers, and formal `Vy` conventions written in Slovak rather than English format?
