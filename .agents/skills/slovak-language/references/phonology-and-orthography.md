# Phonology and Orthography

Sounds and the basics of spelling. Detailed `i/y` rules, selected words, commas, capitalization, and number formatting: [orthography-and-punctuation.md](orthography-and-punctuation.md).

## Alphabet and sound inventory

Alphabetical order: `a á ä b c č d ď dz dž e é f g h ch i í j k l ĺ ľ m n ň o ó ô p q r ŕ s š t ť u ú v w x y ý z ž`. In dictionaries, `ch` is one letter after `h`; `dz` and `dž` are likewise separate letters. Foreign `q, w, x` occur chiefly in names and loans.

Vowels: short `a e i/y o u`; long `á é í/ý ó ú`; `ä` is generally [e] in ordinary standard speech; diphthongs `ia ie iu ô` are phonologically long. `i` and `y` have the same standard pronunciation, but contrast in spelling. `r, ŕ, l, ĺ` may be syllabic: `krk, štvrť, vlk, vŕba`.

Consonant guide: `c` [ts], `č` [tʃ], `dz` [dz], `dž` [dʒ], `š` [ʃ], `ž` [ʒ], `j` [j], `ch` [x], `ľ` [ʎ~lʲ], and `ď ť ň` palatalized. `v` is often [u̯] word-finally or before a consonant. Initial stress is the default: `BRAtislava`, `SLOvensko`; a preceding preposition may join its following word in one stress group.

Voicing assimilates within consonant clusters and final obstruents are devoiced: `dub` ends like [dup]; compare `prosba` with `prosiť`. Preserve spelling in writing; this is pronunciation, not an alternate orthography.

## Softness and i/y spelling

`e, i, í` usually soften preceding `d, t, n` (and historically `l`): `deti, ticho, nikdy`. `ď, ť, ň, ľ` explicitly mark softness before `a, o, u` or consonants: `ďakujem, ťahať, ňou, ľudia`. Do not assume the rule applies in every loanword (`idea, politika`) or after all consonants.

The pedagogical hard/soft/neutral groups help spelling but are not a complete algorithm. When accuracy matters, verify the word form in a reliable Slovak dictionary/corpus rather than inventing `i/y` from sound alone.

## Quantity and rhythmic law

Long vowels/diphthongs carry quantity. The rhythmic law normally prevents two successive long syllables in native Slovak words: `krásny`, `bývam`, `múdry`, rather than forms with a second expected long vowel. It has exceptions (loanwords, some morphological environments, compounds), so apply it as a check, not an unlimited generator.

## Hyphen, apostrophe, and punctuation

Use normal Slovak quotation and punctuation conventions in edited prose. A comma introduces many subordinate clauses: `Viem, že príde.` `Keď prší, zostaneme doma.` Commas also separate coordinated independent clauses when structure/meaning requires it; avoid copying English comma habits.

Use a hyphen in some compound/adjectival constructions and range notation only where standard Slovak requires it. Use an apostrophe mainly for elision in foreign names, not ordinary Slovak inflection. Keep quotation marks and spacing consistent with the surrounding Slovak publication style.

## Capitalization

Capitalize sentence starts, proper names, country/city names, official institution names when used as their full name, and formal address pronouns in direct individual correspondence (`Vy, Vám, Váš`). Days, months, languages, nationalities, and ordinary adjectives are lowercase: `v pondelok`, `v máji`, `po slovensky`, `slovenský`.

## Pronunciation coaching sequence

1. Establish vowel length and first-syllable stress with minimal contrast and recording.
2. Train `c/č`, `s/š`, `z/ž`, `dz/dž`, `ch`, `r`, and soft consonants in words, then phrases.
3. Add syllabic `r/l` and consonant clusters slowly; do not insert English vowels.
4. Train connected speech and final devoicing only after intelligible individual words.
5. Use audio from native sources for feedback; spelling alone cannot validate prosody.
